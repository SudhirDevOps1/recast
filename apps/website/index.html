Capabilities

Built like a studio.
Priced like nothing.

Everything Recast does happens locally. The only network calls are the ones you explicitly approve — an update check, a model download, a missing codec.

Camera

Auto-zoom that reads the room

Interest clustering finds where you click, then critically damped springs fly the camera — subtle 1.25×, balanced 1.6×, punchy 2.0×. Manual keyframes and follow-cursor when you want the wheel.

Cursor

A cursor worth watching

Native or rendered, sized and tinted, with outline, shadow, click ripple and idle fade. Your pointer becomes a narrator instead of a distraction.

Captions

Captions, never uploaded

Whisper runs on your machine. Word-level timing, editable text, burn-in or .srt/.vtt. English and Hindi first.

Local captions, zero uploads.
Presets

Six looks, one click

Frame, padding, background, shadow and cursor style in a single preset. Save any look as a .recaststyle and share it with your team.

Export

Every format that matters

H.264 MP4 by default; HEVC, ProRes, WebM and GIF when you need them. The encoder path is always visible and every file is verified with ffprobe.

HW H.264→SW H.264→FFmpeg
Reliability

Crash-safe by design

Recordings land on disk as two-second segments with an atomic manifest. A crash costs you a fragment, never a take — sessions recover on next launch.

Speed

Keyboard-first, ⌘K everywhere

Space, J/K/L, I/O, S to split, full undo history — plus a command palette that puts every action one keystroke away.

  1. 01

    Record

    Pick a screen, a window or a drawn area with a 4× magnifier. Mic and system audio land on separate tracks, cursor is sampled at 60 Hz in the background, and every two seconds is written safely to disk.

    • Countdown, pause, resume, global hotkey
    • Per-monitor DPI, mixed-DPI aware
    • Up to 4K60 with a dropped-frame counter
  2. 02

    Let Recast cut

    Auto-zoom writes the camera moves from your clicks. Trim with I/O, pick a preset, choose the aspect ratio — the preview is the export, pixel for pixel, because both render through the same compositor.

    • Manual keyframes and follow-cursor mode
    • 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:5, 21:9
    • One undo step per preset change
  3. 03

    Export

    MP4 in H.264 with a size estimate before you commit. Cancel any time without partial files, and an ffprobe verification pass after every render. The encoder path is never hidden from you.

    • Hardware → software → FFmpeg fallback
    • Deterministic frame clock, no dropped renders
    • SHA256SUMS on every release

Under the hood

Four processes.
Zero uploads.

Recast is an Electron shell around four small, boring, well-tested pieces. Each one has a single job and fails on its own without taking the recording with it.

Read docs/architecture.md
  1. 01 — Capture

    The display never leaves the GPU

    Electron's desktopCapturer hands over a live MediaStream; on Wayland the PipeWire portal does the same. Frames are composited and encoded in-process — nothing is uploaded, nothing is cached to a temp server.

    desktopCapturer · getDisplayMedia · 60 Hz cursor sampler
  2. 02 — Segment

    A crash costs you two seconds

    Every fragment is muxed to disk as it arrives, so a crash or a forced quit never costs more than the current segment. On relaunch the library offers the salvage, and the timeline rebuilds from the fragments.

    segment-writer · 2 s fragments · recovery on launch
  3. 03 — Compose

    One compositor, both directions

    Preview and export call the same canvas compositor with the same clock, so a frame cannot look different after you press export. Camera springs, cursor styling and captions are all evaluated at render time.

    canvas 2D · deterministic frame clock · same path for preview + export
  4. 04 — Encode

    Hardware first, and honest about it

    Hardware H.264 when the machine allows it, software next, FFmpeg last — and the encoder used is always printed in the export panel. The finished file is verified with ffprobe before it is handed back to you.

    VideoEncoder → x264 → ffmpeg · ffprobe verify · no partial files
